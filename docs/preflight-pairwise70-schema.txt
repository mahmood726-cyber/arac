Rationale:
- 79.5% of rows carry "AuthorYear" Study labels (e.g. "Smith 2010") which
  are parseable bibliographic identifiers — first-author + year is sufficient
  to query PubMed/Europe PMC for the trial record.
- 1.1% carry explicit NCT IDs directly in the Study field.
- 19.5% carry trial acronyms (e.g. "HYVET", "SUMMIT", "MRC-TMH") that lack
  a first-author name — these require acronym→PubMed lookup or manual mapping.
- No PMID, DOI, or ROR columns exist anywhere in the schema.
- The schema is highly stable (same ~27 cols across all 595 files; newer files
  add ROB2 bias domains but not new identifiers).

=======================================================================
RECOMMENDED DOWNSTREAM METADATA PATH
=======================================================================

Primary key: Study column (Author Year → PubMed lookup)
  - Parse "Lastname YYYY" or "Lastname Initials YYYY" patterns.
  - Query Europe PMC /search?query=auth:{lastname} AND pub_year:{year}
    filtered to clinical trial type.
  - Expect ~80% hit rate on first-author+year. Accept top-1 result when
    title+year+trial type match. Flag ambiguous (>1 candidate) for review.

Secondary key: NCT IDs (1.1% of rows, 47 files)
  - Extract NCT IDs directly where Study matches r'NCT\d{6,8}'.
  - CT.gov /api/query/full_studies?expr=NCT_ID gives country, sponsor,
    and enrollment direct.

Fallback for acronyms (19.5% of rows):
  - Build a lookup table: known acronym → PMID or NCT.
    MRC, HYVET, SHEP, HOPE, PEPI are well-known; ~200 distinct acronyms
    estimated across the corpus.
  - Alternatively: query PubMed with the acronym as title keyword.

Do NOT rely on Footnotes — they contain follow-up durations and footnote
text, not structured identifiers (with 2 trivial exceptions).

The review_doi gives the Cochrane review DOI — useful for fetching the
full review's reference list (Cochrane API or JATS XML) as a second-pass
cross-check on Study→PMID mapping quality.
